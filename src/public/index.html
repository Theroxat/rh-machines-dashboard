What a Machine is

Ten thousand of them, and two things about each are sealed at mint and never change. Its rarity — x1 through x10 — which is read off its headwear. And its desk, one of sixteen, which decides which stock it earns double on.

Weight is the only thing that pays

A Machine with no ink weighs nothing. It earns nothing and it cannot vote. Burning 5,000 $PRINTER switches it on — 4,250 of that becomes ink, 750 is an operating charge — and it then weighs its class. A x2 weighs 2.

weight = class × √( ink ÷ 4,250 )

Burn more and it weighs more, but along a square root, so four times the ink buys twice the weight and nine times buys three. A ceiling common to the whole fleet caps the multiplier, and it rises as the community burns. Past it, a burn is refused rather than quietly clipped.

Everything else follows weight. Your share of every payout is your weight over the fleet's, and you vote with your weight rather than one vote per Machine.

Three states, and they are not interchangeable

Dormantno ink. Earns nothing, votes on nothing.
Litinked during this epoch. It starts earning at the next one — so a Machine bought and switched on today takes no part in the pot that is waiting.
Earningactive for a full epoch. Earns, votes, counted for the drop.

Where the money comes from

Selling $PRINTER costs a fee, and that fee is collected in USDG. Trading the NFTs now feeds it too. The money piles up until a ballot closes, and then it is spent buying stock.

This means the pot and the token price pull in opposite directions: a calm market fills it slowly. It also means the figure on the Market tab is an amount waiting, not a rate — nothing has been paid at a regular interval, and the next drop will be worth whatever the following days of trading put into it.

The vote decides who gets bought

Once an epoch, a ballot opens for 48 hours. Every awake Machine votes with its weight for one of sixteen desks. The pot is then cut between the desks in proportion to the votes they drew — nothing is winner-takes-all, and a desk nobody voted for gets nothing.

Inside each slice, every Machine is paid its weight over the fleet's, and a Machine sitting on that slice's desk counts twice. So you receive every stock that was bought, whatever your desk; your own desk only doubles your share of its own slice. That is the reason to vote your own desk rather than the one you think will win.

A stock the protocol cannot buy — no pool on this chain — has its slice paid out in the winning stock instead, keeping the same structure. Its desk's Machines still double.

The stock lands inside the Machine

Every Machine owns an account of its own, and that is where its stock is deposited. The account belongs to the token, not to you: sell the Machine and whatever is inside goes with it, unless you withdraw first. Buying one that is still loaded means buying its contents too, which is why every listing here shows what it is carrying.

The Anvil

A market maker run by the protocol. It publishes a standing bid — the same amount for any Machine, whatever its rarity — and it pays in $PRINTER, net of the sell fee. It also holds a shelf of Machines it will sell, and a crate you can open for one drawn at random.

Because its bid is denominated in $PRINTER while listings are priced in ETH, the two drift apart whenever the token moves. When the bid climbs above a listing, this page says so.

What this page measures, and what it cannot

Floor pricethe cheapest asking price in a class and state. An asking price is not a price obtained.
Price per weight pointwhat one x1 point of weight costs here. It is the only figure that compares a x1 to a x10, since seven floors side by side price seven different things. A dormant Machine carries its activation.
Best ROIthe pot share plus the stock already inside, over the price. It counts what a buyer receives, not what the Machine will earn later.
Wallet wortheach Machine at the better of its class floor and the Anvil's bid, plus the stock inside, plus what the wallet itself holds.

Where the numbers come from

Weights, ink, desks, the ballot and the pot are read from the chain, not computed here. Listings and pictures come from the public OpenSea API. Token prices come from DexScreener. The book is swept every minute, ink every ten. Anything that cannot be read is shown as unknown rather than as zero — a zero and a failed read are not the same thing, and confusing them is how a dashboard lies quietly.

Two things are derived rather than read: which headwear grants which rarity, and the split between what a Machine earns from each desk's slice. Both are checked against the chain on every boot, and the first is set out below.

Which headwear grants which rarity

A Machine's rarity is sealed on chain, and it always matches the Headwear it wears. This table is derived from the collection itself on every boot, not written down — if a headwear ever appeared under two different rarities, it would be named here rather than filed under one of them.
